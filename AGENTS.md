## Project notes

- Quality Roofing Specialists marketing site: Next.js App Router, JavaScript, plain CSS. See README.md for the layout.
- Blog articles are the exception: they live in the Supabase `posts` table and are edited in the team dashboard at `/admin/` (`app/admin`, `components/admin`, `lib/postsStore.js`, `lib/adminAuth.js`, `lib/articleFormat.js`; table in `scripts/supabase-posts.sql`). The site reads the published ones through a cached request tagged "posts" (pre-built pages, refreshed hourly and instantly after every dashboard save with `revalidateTag`), so a saved change is live in seconds with no deploy. `data/blog/posts.js` is now the starting copy (the dashboard's one-time import) and the fallback when Supabase isn't configured, is down during a build, or the table has no published article; edit articles in the dashboard, not in that file, once the import is done. The dashboard: one shared `ADMIN_PASSWORD` (12+ characters) and a signed 12-hour cookie, every change checks the login and that the request came from the site itself, the site's header, footer, widgets and analytics are left out of `/admin/` (`SiteChrome`), and robots.txt, a meta tag and headers keep it out of search. Articles are written as plain text (`## Heading`, `### Subheading`, `> note`, `- bullets`, `1. steps`, `- [ ] checklist`, `| tables |`, `Q:`/`A:` FAQs: `lib/articleFormat.js`, tested to turn all 13 original articles into text and back unchanged), and the editor runs the keyword rule and wording rules live (the keyword rule blocks publishing). The home page's article grid shows the articles given a spot 1 to 3 in the dashboard, then the newest. The sitemap, `llms.txt`, `llms-full.txt`, the OKF bundle and the chat assistant's page search all read the same article list (`lib/siteIndex.js`). To check the setup on the live site: POST to `/api/lead/` with the `x-leads-diagnostic` header and `{"configCheck": true}` (it reports `articles: ok: N articles, M published`).
- Editable content lives in `data/`; each page section is a component in `components/sections/` with its CSS file beside it. Page layouts (service, hub, city) are in `components/templates/`; `data/catalog.js` holds the page structure and `data/content.js` joins it with the copy.
- SEO rule from the owner: every page's `keyword` must appear in its meta title, meta description, H1 and first 100 words. Content text uses `[words](/path/)` links and `**bold**` (rendered by `components/ui/Rich.jsx`); only link to pages that exist.
- Page titles: `pageMetadata` (`lib/pages.js`) adds " | Quality Roofing Specialists" only when the whole title is 60 characters or less; keep a page's own `metaTitle` to about 60 so the keyword is never cut off. A project page whose headline is longer gets a separate `metaTitle`. Single service pages can set `h1` (e.g. "Roof Repair in Los Angeles & Orange County") when the H1 should say where, while `title` stays short for menus and links; hubs use `hero.heading`. Wording follows real searches from Search Console (`seo-audit/SEARCH-CONSOLE-BASELINE-2026-10-05.md`): people search "roofing contractor Los Angeles", "roof repair Los Angeles", "commercial roofing Los Angeles", so the home page title/H1 (`HOME_TITLE`, `HOME_H1` in `data/site.js`: "Roofing Contractor in Los Angeles & Orange County") and the service H1s name Los Angeles (and Orange County).
- Terminology rule from the owner: homeowners are "homeowners"; property management and commercial jobs are "clients"; QRS employees are "roofers"; subcontracted or outside workers are "crews" (never "subs"/"subcontractors"); general contractors are "contractors". A client's own staff keep their role names ("your property manager or building engineer").
- Stylesheet order matters: `app/layout.js` imports `leaflet/dist/leaflet.css`, then `app/globals.css`, then components import their own CSS. Keep shared primitives (buttons, form fields, `.container`, `.section`) in `globals.css` so section styles can override them.
- Structured data: every page renders one `<JsonLd data={pageJsonLd({...})} />` (`lib/structuredData.js`) from the same title, description, path, crumbs and FAQs as its metadata and sections. Breadcrumb JSON-LD comes from `pageJsonLd`, not from `Breadcrumbs.jsx`.
- `/llms.txt`, `/llms-full.txt` and the OKF v0.2 bundle at `/okf/` are generated from `lib/pageIndex.js` by `lib/aiFiles.js`. New section services and cities are picked up automatically; one-off pages must be added to `PAGE_INDEX` and to `ALL_PATHS` (`data/content.js`).
- `ALLOW_INDEXING` (`lib/seo.js`) switches robots.txt and the robots meta tag to noindex on Vercel preview deployments and when `SITE_NOINDEX=true`; `next.config.mjs` adds `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` on any host other than qualityroofingspecialists.com (e.g. the *.vercel.app URL). `LOAD_TRACKING` (same file) loads Google Analytics (gtag.js, `GA_ID` in `data/site.js`) and Clarity only on indexable production builds. Don't hard-code robots rules elsewhere.
- Metal roofing and TPO: metal roofing is on again (`data/services/metal.js`: hub, standing seam, metal roof repair at `/residential-roofing/metal-roofing/…`; the old `/metal-roofing/…` addresses redirect there) and TPO roofing is a commercial service page (`/commercial-roofing/tpo-roofing/`, in `data/services/commercialServices.js`). The owner confirmed both on 2026-10-05. Rain gutters stay hidden, so the metal copy doesn't mention them. The metal pages have no photos yet (placeholder art), and the lead form's roof types now include Metal.
- Articles on pages: `LatestArticles` (`components/sections/LatestArticles.jsx`) shows three blog cards (picture, date, title, "Read the article") above the FAQ. The home page shows the newest; service, hub, city and county pages show the closest by topic (`lib/articles.js`: a page's topics come from its address, a post's from `topics` in `data/blog/posts.js`, and a post that lists the page in `related` ranks first). Give every new post `topics`, and it shows up on the right pages by itself.
- Blog index (`app/blog/page.js`): the newest post with a picture is large beside the hero heading, then `BlogBrowser` (`components/sections/BlogBrowser.jsx`) shows the filters (Show: Residential/Commercial; Topic: from `lib/blogFilters.js`, matched on each post's `topics`, a filter with no posts is not shown, one that would give no results with the other filter on is greyed out), the cards and the page buttons (9 per page). Every card stays in the page's HTML (the ones not on screen are `hidden`), so all articles remain links for search engines and without JavaScript; the hero article joins the list only when a filter is on, so no thumbnail shows twice. The choice lives in the address (`/blog/?audience=commercial&topic=storm&page=2`). A post without its own `image` yet can set `cardImage` (a stand-in used on cards only, never as the article's picture or in structured data); use a different photo for each so two cards never match, and delete it when the real image is added. A new picture for a live post needs a new file name (30-day image cache).
- Request form: the estimate form that used to sit at the bottom of every page is now the step-by-step form at `/start/` (`components/sections/StartStepper.jsx`, rules and wording in `data/start.js`; `START_PATH` is the one place to change the address). The data files still link to `#roof-check`: `SiteLink` turns that into `/start/`, so keep using it for every Get Pro Advice / Request an Estimate link. The `RoofCheck` section at the bottom of each page is now the offer card plus a start card whose choices open `/start/?need=…`. The steps adapt (urgency question only for repairs, timing for repairs and replacements, role and company for HOA and commercial properties, the commercial offer card for them) and pre-answer from the page the visitor came from (`PageTrail` in `app/layout.js`, `lib/pageTrail.js`). It posts the same fields to `/api/lead` (details go in `message`), then opens `/thank-you/`. On `/start/` the header shows only the logo, theme switch and call button, and the footer only its small print (`HideOnStart`). The page is noindex and not in the sitemap or AI files.
- Supabase: every lead is also saved by `lib/leads.js` as a row of the `leads` table (REST call with `SUPABASE_URL` + `SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY` (an `sb_secret_…` key; `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` also works) from Vercel, nothing in the code; the table, its row level security and the merged `lead_contacts` view are in `scripts/supabase-leads.sql`, run once in the Supabase SQL Editor). It is one more channel next to the email and the sheet: a failure shows in the diagnostic response (`channels.database`) and never stops the others. Never use the publishable or anon key for it. The Supabase skills are installed in `.claude/skills/` (`npx skills add supabase/agent-skills`): load them before changing anything in the database, and check the current docs (append `.md` to a supabase.com/docs URL), because Supabase changes often.
- Preferred visit: the last `/start/` step has an optional date (tomorrow to six months out, set in the browser because the page is built ahead of time) and time (`VISIT_TIMES` in `data/estimateOptions.js`), hidden when water is coming in now. The API keeps only a real date from yesterday to about a year out (`visitDate` in `app/api/lead/route.js`) and a listed time. They are `preferredDate`/`preferredTime` in the lead email, the sheet (two columns added at the end of `scripts/google-sheet-leads.gs`, which must be re-pasted and re-deployed in Apps Script once), Supabase and the confirmation email.
- Confirmation email: a visitor who types an email on the `/start/` form or the Instant Quote gets a short confirmation (`lib/autoReply.js`, wording in `data/autoReply.js`; sent through Resend from the `LEADS_FROM_EMAIL` address after the lead is delivered, replies go to the lead inbox). It mirrors the thank-you page (no response time, no price for the roof evaluation or the commercial roof survey, nothing the site doesn't already say) and has home, HOA/commercial, urgent ("water is coming in now") and Instant Quote versions. It is skipped for chat and AI-agent leads, sent once per address per day, uses only a first name that looks like a name, and `LEADS_AUTOREPLY=off` turns it off. To test it on the live site, POST to `/api/lead/` with the `x-leads-diagnostic` header (the `LEADS_SHEET_SECRET`) and `"autoReplyTest": true` plus a name, phone and email: it sends only that email (subject starts with TEST), with no lead email or sheet row. The Resend key is only on Vercel, not in `.env.local`.
- Campaign attribution: `components/widgets/CampaignWelcome.jsx` (mounted in `app/layout.js`) runs in the browser only, so pages stay static. It saves the link's `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` in sessionStorage (`lib/attribution.js`), and the estimate form, Instant Quote and chat send them with the lead (`app/api/lead`, `app/api/chat` → `lib/leads.js` → email rows and sheet columns). Only the Google Business Profile campaigns listed in `data/campaigns.js` show a welcome card (when `SHOW_WELCOME_CARD` is on; it's off for now, attribution only), with the listed text, never text from the URL.
- Review order by page topic: the review slider (`ReviewSlider.jsx`) and the bottom-left pop-up (`ReviewToast.jsx`) show every review but lead with the ones closest to the page's topic (`lib/reviewTopics.js`: a review's topics are found from its words, or set with `topics: [...]` on the review in `data/reviews.js`; a page's topics come from its address in `lib/pageTopics.js`, the same table the related articles use). A tile page starts with tile reviews, repair pages with leak and repair ones, and a city page (`places` = city and neighborhoods) with the reviews that name them; a page with no match, the home page and commercial pages (no commercial review yet) keep the usual order. The pop-up asks the slider for a review by name (`SHOW_REVIEW_EVENT`), so both can sort differently. Live Google reviews are sorted the same way in the browser.
- Live reviews: `components/sections/ReviewSlider.jsx` and the bottom-left `ReviewToast.jsx` share one `/api/google-reviews/` request per page load (`lib/liveReviews.js`; Places API, `lib/placesReviews.js`, `data/places.js`) and show 5-star reviews with Google's required credits; with no `GOOGLE_PLACES_KEY` or Place IDs, or on any error, it keeps the hand-picked `data/reviews.js`. Google's rules don't allow storing reviews: never cache that response (no `revalidate`, no storage).
- Page speed: photos are served as AVIF (WebP as fallback) in finer width steps (`images` in `next.config.mjs`; `qualities: [60, 75]`, the card photos use 60) and `/images/*` is cached for 30 days, so a photo replaced under the same file name takes up to 30 days to refresh for returning visitors: give a new photo a new file name (or bump `CACHE_RESET`). Both fonts are variable fonts (no `weight` list in `app/layout.js`), which keeps the stylesheet at 19 font rules instead of 83. Draw arrows, checks and stars (→ ✓ ★) in `var(--font-glyph)` (the `.arrow` class, or that variable on the element), never in the web font (it was Open Sans when this was measured): those characters fall in the font's "symbols" and "math" ranges, and the browser then downloads two extra font files (69 KB) before painting any page that shows one. Measured with Lighthouse on production builds (`next build`, a scratch copy, mobile settings): `experimental.inlineCss` makes the HTML 3 times bigger (the CSS is sent twice) and does not improve the score, and `cssChunking: 'graph'` does not reduce the 19 CSS requests, so neither is used. Don't give a `<script>` an `id` that is a global the script needs: `id="clarity"` became `window.clarity` and kept Clarity from ever starting. Keep heavy code out of the first load. Google Analytics and Clarity load with `strategy="lazyOnload"` (`app/layout.js`); the Instant Quote (`InstantQuoteLoader`), the photo slider's WebGL engine (`morphEngine.js`) and the service-area map's Leaflet start only when needed or in view; the header uses the 12 KB `qrs-logo-dark-sm.webp` (the footer keeps the full-size logo).
- Service area map (`ServiceArea.jsx`, on the home, Service Areas and Contact pages): ZIP check, the Los Angeles and Orange County borders plus the Inland Empire (western Riverside County: Corona, Riverside, Menifee, Temecula, cut off at longitude -117.03; and the southwest corner of San Bernardino County between Pomona and Riverside, cut at longitude -117.25 and latitude 34.17) outlined (`data/serviceAreaOutline.js`, Census data), and a link to every city page below the map (the Inland Empire has no city pages, so no links). On the home page (`hideCities`) those city links are in the page's HTML but never shown, not even after a visitor searches a ZIP code (the owner doesn't want a list under the map; a plain "View all service areas" text link to `/service-areas/` sits under the map instead, `allAreasLink`), so they still count as links for search engines but weigh less; the Service Areas and Contact pages show them straight away. The ZIP check answers "in our service area" (within 15 miles of a city), "inside the area we serve, call to confirm" (inside the outline) or "outside". The Inland Empire is on the map only: the structured data (`SERVICE_COUNTIES`), llms.txt, the chat assistant and the page copy still say Los Angeles and Orange County. `ProjectMap.jsx` still uses the older `.loc-list` styles in `ServiceArea.css`.
- Hero headings carry the keyword and a "dream outcome": the H1 is the keyword line (as before) plus a short result line in gold inside the same H1 (`outcome` prop of `Hero`; all the lines are in one file, `data/heroOutcomes.js`, keyed by page address; the home page's big headline "Come home to a roof you can trust." is in `app/page.js`, and there the keyword line above it is the H1). Write an outcome as the end state the visitor wants (a dry home, a business that keeps running) in plain words, short enough for two lines, never a guarantee or a number (no "leak-free", "forever", years or prices) and nothing the page doesn't already say. City page headings are just the keyword line ("Santa Monica roofing"); the promise is in the outcome line. A new page needs its line added there, or it simply shows the keyword line alone.
- Hero layout is compact: the headline is about 56px on desktop (46px tablet, 30-38px phone) with a soft shade from the left behind the copy, the intro and buttons are smaller, and heroes are 440-560px tall (the home hero is deliberately taller, `hero-top-pad`: 720-860px with more space above the copy). The breadcrumb trail (`crumbs`) sits in the eyebrow's place above the headline, the current page in gold, and there is no separate breadcrumb bar under the hero; the `eyebrow` text only shows on a page with no trail (the home page, where it is the H1 keyword line, and the thank-you page). The four proof points are a slim navy strip directly under the hero (`stats` on `Hero` renders `ProofBar strip`) on every page that shows them: home, hubs, services, plans, residential, cities, counties, about, careers, contact, contractors and service areas; blog, reviews and projects have none. All sizes are in `components/sections/Hero.css` and the strip in `ProofBar.css`.
- Fonts and underlined words: every heading (h1-h6) is Big Shoulders (`--font-display`, uppercase, weight 800; 700 for headings of 17px or less) and the body, menus, buttons and labels are Libre Franklin (`--font-text`, `--font-body`, `--font-head`, `--font-btn`); both are variable Google fonts set with `next/font` in `app/layout.js` (Roboto Condensed and Open Sans are gone). Big Shoulders has no size-adjusted fallback in `next/font` (the build prints "Failed to find font override values"), so check CLS after touching it. Product names (HardShell, TotalShield, FlatGuard, LeakRescue, RoofScan 360, SecondLife, RoofCare Plan, ReserveReady: `PRODUCT_BRANDS` in `components/ui/Rich.jsx`) keep their serif italic (`.product-brand`, Georgia) exactly as before. A heading can underline words (a thick gold line, `h1 u,h2 u,h3 u` in `app/globals.css`): write `__word__` in a heading rendered through `components/ui/Mark.jsx` (Faq, LatestArticles, WhyQrs, QrsStandard, Process, FinalCta, DifferenceBand, ServiceArea, ServicesCarousel, RoofCheck, Testimonials; `Mark` leaves a string with no marks alone), or a `<u>` in JSX (the home hero's "trust."). One word per heading, never a whole line. `plainText`/`toMarkdown`/`unmark` (`lib/richText.js`) strip the marks for structured data, the AI files and the chat assistant; page titles and meta descriptions are written without them.
- Headings are centered site-wide: every `h1` and `h2` on a page, the gold `.eyebrow` kick line above one, the breadcrumbs, `.section-head` and the lead-in line under a heading (`main h1,main h2,main .eyebrow` in `app/globals.css`), and every `Hero` is centered by default (`align` defaults to `'center'`; `align="left"` still gives the old left-justified hero). The `h3` titles inside cards stay left with their card's text. When you add a section, don't left-align its heading.
- Buttons: every `.btn` (`app/globals.css`) is a pill with depth (gradient, light top edge, layered shadow, a sheen sweep on hover, lift, leaning arrow, pressed state); the header's theme and menu buttons and the assistant bubble use the same look. Don't set a flat `box-shadow` or background on a `.btn` in a component. White buttons (`.btn-line`, `.btn-light`) become dark glass in the dark theme (`app/dark-theme.css`), because their text color is `--navy`, which is light there.
- Mobile first: base CSS is for phones and `min-width` queries add to it (keep it that way; avoid new `max-width` queries). On phones a slim action bar (`components/widgets/MobileActionBar.jsx`: Call and Get Pro Advice) slides in after the hero, steps aside while the request form shows and is hidden on `/start/`, `/thank-you/` and from 901px up; it sets `--bar-h` on `<html>` and the chat bubble, review pop-up and cookie notice lift by it. Hover effects (`.btn`) are inside `@media (hover:hover)` so they don't stick after a tap; small links get an invisible 44px hit area (see the touch rules under `button{...}` in `app/globals.css`); filter chips, slider arrows and the cookie button are 44px; the map's zoom buttons are 42px on touch screens. Check new pages at 390px.
- Corners: `--radius` (16px: cards, panels, photos) and `--radius-sm` (12px: form fields, icon boxes, avatars, small chips, thumbnails) are the two tokens in `app/globals.css`; pill buttons stay 999px. Use them instead of a pixel value. A stretched-link or focus-ring overlay on a card uses the card's own token.
- Aurora backgrounds: the "Why Choose QRS" band (`WhyQrs.jsx`) and the footer (`Footer.jsx`, left out on /start/) draw a slow WebGL aurora over a navy night sky and a row of rooftops (`components/sections/AuroraBackground.jsx`, shader in `auroraEngine.js`, plain WebGL, no libraries; the engine is fetched only when the section is about to scroll into view, draws at 0.6 scale and 30 fps only while on screen, and stands still for reduced motion). `variant="section"` is the bright gold-curtain look, `variant="footer"` a calmer dusk (violet and blue curtains, warm horizon glow, denser rooftops) so the links stay readable. The colors come from `--gold` and `--plum` (the engine has matching defaults). Before it draws, without WebGL, with Data Saver and in print, the CSS poster in `AuroraBackground.css` (gradients plus two SVG rooftop tiles) shows instead. The parent needs `position:relative; isolation:isolate; overflow:hidden`; the readability scrim is the parent's `:before`.
- Scroll reveals: `components/ui/RevealSections.jsx` (switch: `SCROLL_REVEALS` in `data/promo.js`) fades the parts of each page up a few pixels, once, as they scroll into view (the heading, text, cards of a grid a moment apart, images). It only starts after the page has loaded, marks only parts still below the fold (`data-reveal`, `html.reveal-on`, rules in `app/globals.css`), removes the mark when a part has appeared, leaves the hero, the proof strip, sliders, maps and anything with `data-no-reveal` alone, and does nothing for reduced motion. Opacity and transform only, so no layout shift. The old every-h2 fade-up (`PAGE_ENTRANCES`) stays off.

From 1366 to 1499px it uses the short labels "Contractors" and "Estimate"; from 1500px the full "For Contractors" and "Get Pro Advice".
